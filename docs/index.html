📐Formal Mathematical Framework of Counterfactual Patient Migration (CPM)

Traditional counterfactual models only report a change in predicted probability (delta y). The CPM framework provides a rigorous, topology-aware mathematical formulation that explains how a patient navigates the clinical similarity network, decouples structural peer movement from intervention effort, and prevents false confidence from static graph assumptions.

Equation 1: Domain-Weighted Clinical Distance Similarity Metric
d_clinical(x, x') = √ [ Σ w_k · ((x_k - x'_k) / σ_k)² ]
Clinical Justification & Real-Life Usecase: Standard Euclidean distance treats all features equally (e.g., 10 mmHg of blood pressure equals 10 years of age). In clinical cardiology, arterial hypertension (w = 1.8) and hypercholesterolemia (w = 2.0) carry significantly higher lethal risk than gender (w = 0.5). Domain weighting guarantees that the constructed patient graph reflects physiological hazard rather than statistical noise.
Equation 2: GraphSAGE Multi-Head Aggregation Deep Representation
h_v^(l) = σ [ W^(l) · ( h_v^(l-1) || AGG({ h_u^(l-1), ∀ u ∈ N(v) }) ) ]
Clinical Justification & Real-Life Usecase: In medicine, a patient's vulnerability is contextualized by their peer group. GraphSAGE aggregates latent clinical features from the 15 nearest clinical neighbors across 2 neural message-passing layers. This ensures the model learns non-linear risk representations from community health patterns rather than treating the patient as an isolated tabular record.
Equation 3: Jaccard Neighbor Turnover (T) Network Relocation
T = 1 - ( | N_orig ∩ N_new | / | N_orig ∪ N_new | ) ∈ [0, 1]
Clinical Justification & Real-Life Usecase: When a patient lowers their blood pressure, do they actually break away from their high-risk cardiac peers? If T = 0, the patient remains trapped among the same sick individuals despite taking pills. When T = 1.0, all 15 baseline neighbors are replaced by healthy individuals, confirming genuine clinical relocation.
Equation 4: Neighborhood Prevalence Shift (R) Peer Risk Shift
R = max( 0, ( 1/|N_orig| · Σ y_u ) - ( 1/|N_new| · Σ y_v ) )
Clinical Justification & Real-Life Usecase: Directly measures the disease density of the patient's immediate social and biological cohort. For Ramesh, peer CVD incidence plummets from 94.4% down to 6.7%, yielding R = 0.8778. This offers doctors an intuitive way to explain outcomes: "Your community risk dropped by 88%."
Equation 5: GraphSAGE Latent Displacement (D_norm) Manifold Movement
D = || z_new - z_orig ||_2, D_norm = (D - D_min) / (D_max - D_min) ∈ [0, 1]
Clinical Justification & Real-Life Usecase: Feature space Euclidean distance can be misleading because physiological features have non-linear interactions. D_norm measures displacement in the deep 32-dimensional latent manifold constructed by GraphSAGE, verifying that the patient has moved into the low-risk geometric basin.
Equation 6: Approved Composite CPM Metric Core Paper Metric
CPM = 0.40 · T + 0.40 · R + 0.20 · D_norm
Clinical Justification & Real-Life Usecase: Validated through sensitivity analysis (rank correlation r > 0.94). Weights allocate 40% to topology replacement, 40% to peer disease reduction, and 20% to latent manifold movement. Feature effort F_norm = ||Δx||_2 / √d is reported separately to prevent hiding difficult interventions behind a high score.
